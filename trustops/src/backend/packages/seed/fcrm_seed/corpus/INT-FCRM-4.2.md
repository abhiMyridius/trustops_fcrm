# Internal FCRM Policy 4.2 · High-Risk Corridor Jurisdictions (synthetic corpus v14)

> Synthetic internal standard for demonstration. Not the policy of any real institution.

## Section 1 Scope

### FCRM42-1.1 Products and payment flows in scope

The bank shall apply this policy to every product, service and payment flow that sends funds to, receives funds from, or routes funds through a jurisdiction on the internal high-risk corridor list. This includes account transfers, cross-border wires, card programmes and digital wallet services. Business owners shall confirm corridor exposure before launch and whenever a material change alters the destinations or origins a product can reach.

### FCRM42-1.2 Customers with corridor jurisdiction links

The bank shall treat a customer as within scope when their residence, place of incorporation, principal operations or beneficial ownership connects them to a jurisdiction on the high-risk corridor list. Onboarding teams shall record each such link in the customer file and apply the enhanced review steps set out in this policy. Links shall be reassessed at each periodic review and after any change in ownership or activity.

### FCRM42-1.3 Changes and third parties that trigger assessment

The bank shall require a corridor exposure assessment before it enters a new market, onboards a vendor or partner that handles customer transactions, or changes a process that affects how corridor transactions are screened, approved or monitored. The Financial Crime Risk Management function shall maintain the corridor list, publish updates to business lines, and record which assessments were completed against each version.

## Section 2 Jurisdiction rating

### FCRM42-2.1 Jurisdiction Risk Rating Tiers

The bank shall assign every country and payment corridor in which it operates or transacts to one of four rating tiers: low, medium, high, or prohibited. The rating shall reflect inherent financial crime exposure before mitigating controls are considered. Each tier shall have a written definition, and the Financial Crime Risk Management function shall maintain a single authoritative register so that all business lines apply the same rating to the same jurisdiction.

### FCRM42-2.2 Required Inputs to Jurisdiction Ratings

The bank shall base each jurisdiction rating on a documented set of inputs, including the strength of the local anti-money-laundering framework, exposure to sanctions and terrorist financing, corruption and organised crime indicators, transparency of corporate ownership, and the quality of information available from local authorities. The bank shall record the sources and weighting used, so that an independent reviewer can reproduce the resulting rating from the evidence on file.

### FCRM42-2.3 Rating Review Cycle and Event Triggers

The bank shall review every jurisdiction rating at least once a year, and high-rated jurisdictions at least every six months. The bank shall also perform an out-of-cycle review whenever a material event occurs, such as a change in international sanctions posture, a public assessment identifying serious deficiencies, a coup or conflict, or an unusual rise in suspicious activity reports tied to a corridor. Review outcomes and dates shall be logged in the register.

### FCRM42-2.4 Rating Overrides and Approval Authority

The bank shall permit a departure from the calculated jurisdiction rating only where a documented business and risk rationale exists. A downgrade of risk to a lower tier shall require approval from the Chief Compliance Officer, while an upgrade may be applied immediately by the Financial Crime Risk Management function. Every override shall carry an expiry date no later than twelve months out, and the bank shall report all active overrides quarterly to the risk committee.

### FCRM42-2.5 Applying Ratings to Launches and Vendors

The bank shall check the jurisdiction register before launching any product, feature, or customer segment that touches a new country or corridor, and before onboarding any vendor operating or hosting services there. A high rating shall require enhanced due diligence and senior sign-off before go-live, and a prohibited rating shall bar the activity. Launch files shall record the rating relied upon and the date it was confirmed.

## Section 3 Screening and EDD

### FCRM42-3.2 Periodic Re-Rating of Corridor Jurisdictions

The bank shall review the risk rating of every corridor jurisdiction at least once each year and whenever a material event occurs, such as a change in sanctions posture, a published mutual evaluation outcome, or a sharp rise in internal alerts. Any change in rating shall be approved by the Financial Crime Risk Committee, recorded with its rationale, and communicated to screening, monitoring and onboarding teams before taking effect.

### FCRM42-3.3 Source of Funds Corroboration for High Corridors

Where a customer sends or receives funds through a corridor rated High, the bank shall obtain and corroborate the stated source of funds and, for higher-value relationships, the source of wealth. Corroboration shall rely on independent documents or reliable third-party data rather than customer declarations alone, and any unexplained gap shall be escalated to the EDD analyst before the relationship proceeds or expands.

### FCRM42-3.4 Senior Approval for High Corridor Relationships

The bank shall require written approval from a designated senior compliance officer before opening or continuing a relationship whose expected activity is concentrated in High-rated corridors. The approver shall consider the EDD file, the business rationale for the corridor exposure, and any residual concerns, and may impose conditions such as lower limits, closer monitoring, or a shortened review cycle.

### FCRM42-3.5 Jurisdiction Data Provider Due Diligence

The bank shall perform due diligence before onboarding any vendor that supplies country risk lists, sanctions data, or geographic screening tools used for corridor decisions. Assessment shall cover data sources, update frequency, coverage of alternative place names, and audit rights. The bank shall test the vendor's output against a sample of known cases before go-live and at each material product release.

### FCRM42-3.6 Corridor-Specific Monitoring Scenarios and Tuning

The bank shall maintain transaction monitoring scenarios tailored to High-rated corridors, including unusual velocity, structured sending patterns, and routing through intermediary jurisdictions to obscure the destination. Thresholds shall be validated on a regular schedule using alert outcomes, and any new corridor or payment feature shall be assessed for scenario coverage before launch so that no monitoring gap arises.

## Section 4 Reporting

### FCRM42-4.1 Monthly Corridor Exposure Reporting to Committee

The bank shall produce a monthly exposure report for every designated high-risk corridor jurisdiction and deliver it to the financial crime risk committee. The report shall show transaction volumes, value, customer counts, alert rates and open investigations by corridor, and shall highlight material movements against the prior period. Business line owners shall explain any unusual change in writing, and the committee shall record the decisions taken in response.

### FCRM42-4.2 Timelines for Corridor-Linked Suspicious Activity Referrals

The bank shall require that any employee who identifies potentially suspicious activity involving a high-risk corridor jurisdiction refer it to the financial intelligence unit within one business day. The unit shall assess each referral, document the rationale for filing or not filing a report, and complete its decision within the internal timeframe set for corridor cases. Referrals that exceed this timeframe shall be escalated to the head of financial crime compliance with an explanation of the delay.

### FCRM42-4.3 Reporting Changes to Corridor Designations

The bank shall notify all affected business lines, operations teams and compliance staff whenever a jurisdiction is added to, or removed from, the high-risk corridor list. Each affected business line shall report within an agreed period on the customers, products and payment flows touched by the change, together with the control adjustments it intends to make. Compliance shall track these reports to completion and retain the record of each designation decision and its supporting analysis.

### FCRM42-4.4 Quarterly Board Reporting on Corridor Control Effectiveness

The bank shall report to the board risk committee each quarter on the effectiveness of controls over high-risk corridor jurisdictions. The report shall summarise results of control testing, outstanding audit and regulatory findings, remediation progress, and any vendor-supplied data or screening tools that failed to meet quality standards. Where testing reveals material weaknesses, management shall present a dated remediation plan and shall report progress against it until the weakness is closed.
