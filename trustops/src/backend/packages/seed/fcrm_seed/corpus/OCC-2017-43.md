# OCC Bulletin 2017-43 (summarised) (synthetic corpus v14)

> Synthetic stand-in for demonstration. This is not the text of the named publication and does not cite it.

## Due diligence

### OCC43-1.1 Tracking conditions attached to approvals

Banks should record every condition attached to the approval of a new product or feature, assign each condition a named owner and a target completion date, and confirm closure with evidence. Open conditions should be reported to senior risk committees at regular intervals. Where a condition cannot be met on time, the bank should document the reason, apply interim controls, and reassess whether the original approval remains appropriate.

### OCC43-1.2 Refreshing vendor due diligence after onboarding

Banks should not treat vendor due diligence as complete at the point of onboarding. After approval, the bank should refresh its review of the provider when the scope of services expands, when ownership or key subcontractors change, or when performance and control findings suggest elevated exposure. Follow-up reviews should test whether the provider still meets the conditions set at approval and should be documented and retained for supervisory examination.

### OCC43-1.3 Early-life review of newly approved segments

When a bank approves service to a new customer segment, it should schedule a follow-up review of early account activity against the expected profile recorded during due diligence. Material deviations in volumes, counterparties or funding sources should prompt enhanced scrutiny and, where needed, tighter limits or revised onboarding criteria. Findings should be shared with the business line and compliance so that segment assumptions are corrected promptly.

### OCC43-1.4 Follow-up assessment after geographic expansion

Banks that expand into a new geography should plan a structured post-approval assessment of the risks identified during due diligence. The assessment should examine whether local conditions, transaction patterns and available data sources match earlier assumptions, and whether required controls are operating effectively. Where gaps emerge, the bank should restrict further expansion until remediation is verified and the responsible oversight body has reviewed the outcome.

## Approval

### OCC43-2.1 Recording conditions attached to approval

Banks should record every condition attached to the approval of a new product, feature or activity in a single approval file. Each condition should state its purpose, a named accountable owner, a completion deadline and the evidence that will show it has been met. Conditions that are vague or have no owner tend to lapse unnoticed, so approvers should decline to close an approval until the conditions are specific enough to be tested later.

### OCC43-2.2 Scheduled follow-up review after launch

Banks should schedule a follow-up review after an approved change goes live, rather than treating approval as the end of oversight. The review should compare actual customer mix, transaction volumes, alert levels and control performance with what was assumed at approval. Where results differ meaningfully from those assumptions, the bank should document the reasons, adjust controls as needed, and report the outcome to the committee that granted the original approval.

### OCC43-2.3 Escalating unmet conditions and material changes

Banks should escalate promptly when a condition of approval is missed or when the approved activity changes materially after launch, for example through a new vendor, a wider customer base or an added geography. Escalation should go to the original approving body, which may extend a deadline, require added controls, limit activity or withdraw approval. Decisions and their rationale should be recorded so that supervisors and internal audit can trace them.

## Post-launch monitoring

### OCC43-3.1 Tracking approval conditions through to closure

Banks should record every condition attached to a new initiative approval in a central register, assign a named owner and a target completion point to each item, and verify closure with evidence rather than self-attestation. Items that remain open beyond their target should be escalated to the approving committee, which should decide whether to extend, add compensating controls, or suspend the activity until the gap is resolved.

### OCC43-3.2 Early-life review of monitoring scenarios

Banks should conduct a focused review of transaction monitoring coverage shortly after a new offering goes live, comparing actual customer behaviour with the assumptions made at approval. Alert volumes, false-positive rates and unexpected activity patterns should be analysed, and scenario thresholds or data feeds should be tuned where the observed experience shows that the original design does not detect the intended typologies.

### OCC43-3.3 Ongoing oversight of newly engaged third parties

Banks should continue to oversee a newly engaged service provider once it begins performing activities for the bank. Oversight should include periodic review of service-level results, control testing outcomes, complaint and incident trends, and the provider's handling of customer due diligence tasks. Deficiencies should be documented, remediated within agreed timeframes, and considered when deciding whether to renew, restrict or terminate the relationship.

### OCC43-3.4 Reassessing risk when the profile drifts

Banks should define measurable indicators that show when the risk profile of an approved activity has moved materially from what was assessed at approval, such as growth in volume, new customer types, or exposure to additional countries. When indicators are breached, management should repeat the risk assessment, update required controls, and seek fresh approval before the activity is allowed to expand further.
