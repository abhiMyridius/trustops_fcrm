# Internal Third-Party AML Standard 7.1 (synthetic corpus v14)

> Synthetic internal standard for demonstration. Not the policy of any real institution.

## Section 1 Scope

### TP71-1.1 Third parties covered by this standard

The bank shall apply this standard to every third party that performs, supports, or materially influences a financial crime control on the bank's behalf, including providers of customer identification, sanctions screening, transaction monitoring technology, and data used in risk decisions. Business owners shall register each such provider before contracting begins, and the compliance function shall confirm whether the engagement falls within scope before any onboarding activity starts.

### TP71-1.2 Subcontractors and affiliates within scope

This standard shall extend to subcontractors and intragroup affiliates that handle bank customer data or carry out control activities delegated by a primary vendor. The bank shall require each primary vendor to disclose its subcontracting arrangements and shall treat an undisclosed delegation as a breach of contract terms. Affiliates shall be held to the same expectations as external providers unless the compliance function documents an approved exception with a stated expiry.

### TP71-1.3 Events that bring a vendor into scope

The bank shall apply this standard at onboarding of a new vendor, at renewal, and whenever a material change occurs, such as a new service, a new jurisdiction of operation, a change of ownership, or a change in the systems used to deliver the service. Business owners shall notify the compliance function of such changes promptly, and the function shall decide whether the vendor's risk assessment must be refreshed.

## Section 2 Payout agents

### TP71-2.1 Ownership and Control Due Diligence for Agents

The bank shall complete documented due diligence on every prospective payout agent before any activity begins. Due diligence shall identify the agent's beneficial owners, directors and controlling persons, confirm its licensing or registration status, and screen all identified parties against sanctions and adverse media sources. Onboarding shall not proceed where ownership cannot be verified, and the Financial Crime Compliance function shall record approval or rejection with reasons.

### TP71-2.2 Mandatory AML Terms in Agent Contracts

The bank shall not engage a payout agent unless the written agreement contains explicit anti-money laundering obligations. The agreement shall require the agent to maintain a compliance programme, report unusual activity to the bank promptly, cooperate with investigations, and grant the bank and its auditors access to relevant records and premises. Agreements shall also permit the bank to suspend activity immediately where a material control failure is identified.

### TP71-2.4 Restrictions on Agent Sub-Delegation

The bank shall prohibit a payout agent from delegating any part of its contracted activity to a sub-agent or affiliate unless the bank has approved the arrangement in writing in advance. Any approved sub-agent shall be subject to the same due diligence, screening and contractual obligations as the primary agent, and the primary agent shall remain fully accountable to the bank for the conduct and controls of every party it uses.

### TP71-2.5 Ongoing Monitoring of Agent Performance

The bank shall monitor each payout agent throughout the relationship using defined risk indicators. Indicators shall include volumes compared with expected profiles, rates of unusual activity reports, screening alert handling times, complaint patterns and findings from reviews. Results shall be reported to the responsible governance committee at least quarterly, and agents breaching agreed thresholds shall be placed on a remediation plan with assigned owners and deadlines.

### TP71-2.6 Agent Staff Training and Annual Attestation

The bank shall require each payout agent to train all relevant staff on financial crime obligations before they handle bank-related activity and at least once every year thereafter. Training shall cover recognising suspicious behaviour, sanctions awareness and escalation routes. Each agent shall provide training records and an annual written attestation signed by a senior officer, and the bank shall treat missing evidence as a control breach requiring follow-up.

### TP71-2.7 Suspension and Exit of Payout Agents

The bank shall maintain a documented procedure for suspending and terminating payout agents whose conduct or controls present unacceptable financial crime risk. Triggers shall include sanctions exposure, repeated reporting failures, obstruction of reviews and adverse regulatory findings. On exit, the bank shall preserve records, settle open items under supervision, assess whether regulatory reporting is required, and record lessons learned for future agent selection.

## Section 3 Agent KYC standard

### TP71-3.1 Verifying agent identity and legal existence

The bank shall verify the identity and legal existence of every agent before any agreement is signed or any activity is permitted. For corporate agents, the bank shall obtain certified formation documents, confirm the registered address and good standing with an independent source, and record the nature of the agent's business. Individual agents shall be verified against unexpired government-issued identification. No agent shall be activated on the basis of self-declared information alone, and any unresolved discrepancy shall be escalated to the relationship owner and compliance.

### TP71-3.2 Beneficial ownership and control of agents

The bank shall identify and verify the natural persons who own or control each agent entity, including anyone holding a significant ownership share and at least one individual who exercises senior management control. Where ownership runs through holding companies, trusts or nominee arrangements, the bank shall trace the structure to its ultimate individuals. If the structure cannot be understood or evidenced to the bank's satisfaction, the agent shall not be onboarded, and the decision and its rationale shall be recorded.

### TP71-3.3 Screening agents and their principals

The bank shall screen each agent, its beneficial owners, directors and key personnel against sanctions lists, politically exposed person data and adverse media before onboarding and on an ongoing basis thereafter. Potential matches shall be reviewed by trained analysts and resolved within defined timeframes, with the outcome documented. Where a confirmed sanctions match arises, the bank shall immediately suspend all dealings with the agent and follow the escalation path set by the financial crime compliance function.

### TP71-3.4 Assessing agent risk by location and model

The bank shall assign each agent a risk rating that reflects the countries in which it operates, the customer types it introduces, the products it supports and the way it interacts with customers. Agents based in or serving higher-risk locations, or using remote-only introduction models, shall receive enhanced due diligence, including senior compliance approval and a documented review of expected activity volumes. The rating methodology shall be approved by compliance and reviewed at least annually.

### TP71-3.5 Periodic and event-driven agent reviews

The bank shall refresh agent due diligence on a schedule set by risk rating, with higher-risk agents reviewed more frequently than lower-risk ones. Reviews shall also be triggered by events such as a change of ownership, adverse media, unusual referral patterns, regulatory action against the agent, or a material expansion of its services. Each review shall confirm that documents remain current, that screening is up to date and that the risk rating is still appropriate, with results retained in the agent file.

### TP71-3.6 Agent accountability and exit for KYC failures

The bank shall require every agent to contractually commit to the bank's customer identification standards, to complete initial and annual training, and to cooperate with audits and information requests. Where an agent repeatedly submits incomplete or inaccurate customer information, or refuses to remediate findings, the bank shall restrict its permissions and may terminate the relationship. Terminations for financial crime concerns shall be reported to compliance so that suspicious activity reporting obligations can be assessed.

## Section 4 Contract clauses

### TP71-4.1 Audit and inspection rights in vendor contracts

The bank shall include in every vendor contract that supports a financial-crime control a right for the bank to audit, inspect and test the vendor's relevant systems, records and personnel. Audit rights shall be exercisable on reasonable notice, and without notice where the bank has reason to suspect a control failure. The vendor shall give auditors timely access and shall remediate findings within the period the bank sets. The bank shall retain the right to share audit results with its supervisors.

### TP71-4.2 Supervisory cooperation and information requests

The bank shall require each vendor to cooperate with supervisory authorities that have oversight of the bank. Contracts shall oblige the vendor to answer information requests promptly, to produce documents in a usable format, and to make knowledgeable staff available for interviews. The vendor shall not withhold information on the grounds of commercial confidentiality where the bank needs it to meet a supervisory request. The bank shall set response deadlines in the contract and track compliance with them.

### TP71-4.3 Restrictions on subcontracting and onward delegation

The bank shall prohibit a vendor from subcontracting or delegating any financial-crime control activity without the bank's prior written approval. Where approval is given, the contract shall bind the subcontractor to the same obligations on screening, monitoring, record keeping and reporting that apply to the vendor. The vendor shall remain fully accountable for the subcontractor's performance and shall disclose any change in the subcontracting chain before it takes effect. The bank shall reassess risk whenever the chain changes.

### TP71-4.4 Escalation of suspicious activity by vendors

The bank shall require each vendor that handles customer activity to escalate suspicious or unusual activity to the bank's financial-crime unit within a defined period stated in the contract. The vendor shall record the facts observed, preserve supporting evidence and refrain from informing the customer or any third party of the escalation. Contracts shall name the responsible contacts on both sides and require the vendor to confirm in writing that its staff have been trained on these duties each year.

### TP71-4.5 Record retention and data availability obligations

The bank shall ensure that vendor contracts require the retention of customer identification, transaction and monitoring records for at least the period set in the bank's retention schedule. The vendor shall store records securely, keep them retrievable within an agreed time and provide them to the bank on request in a readable form. On expiry or termination, the vendor shall return or transfer all records to the bank and shall destroy remaining copies only on the bank's written instruction.

### TP71-4.6 Termination rights for compliance failures

The bank shall include in each vendor contract a right to suspend or terminate the arrangement, without penalty to the bank, where the vendor materially breaches its financial-crime obligations, fails to remediate audit findings on time, or becomes subject to a sanctions designation or serious enforcement action. The contract shall provide for an orderly transition so that controls continue to operate during exit. The bank shall document the trigger events and maintain a tested exit plan for critical vendors.
