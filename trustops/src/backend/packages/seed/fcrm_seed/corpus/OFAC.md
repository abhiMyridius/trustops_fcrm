# OFAC sanctions programme requirements (summarised) (synthetic corpus v14)

> Synthetic stand-in for demonstration. This is not the text of the named publication and does not cite it.

## Screening obligations

### OFAC-1.1 Screening customers and owners at onboarding

Banks should screen every prospective customer, and the beneficial owners, controllers and authorised signatories associated with that customer, against applicable sanctions lists before an account or product is opened. Screening should also consider ownership structures in which blocked persons hold a significant combined interest, even where no single listed party is a named owner. Where screening cannot be completed, the relationship should not proceed until the open questions are resolved and documented.

### OFAC-1.2 OFAC Sanctions Screening Standard

…screen all parties to a cross-border payment against the SDN and sectoral lists at initiation and on list update…

### OFAC-1.3 Alert review and escalation discipline

Banks should maintain written procedures for reviewing potential sanctions matches so that each alert is assessed consistently, promptly and by suitably trained staff. Reviewers should record the reasoning behind every disposition, including the identifiers compared and the evidence relied upon. Alerts that cannot be confidently discounted should be escalated to a designated sanctions specialist, and transactions should be held while that escalation is pending. Management should monitor alert ageing and backlog levels to make sure delays do not weaken the control.

### OFAC-1.4 Calibration and testing of screening tools

Banks should calibrate name-matching logic, fuzzy thresholds and transliteration handling to the risks of their own business, and should test that calibration before deployment and at regular intervals afterwards. Testing should use representative samples of known true matches and realistic variations to show that relevant hits are not missed. Changes to rules, data feeds or screening software, including those supplied by third parties, should be approved, validated and documented, with results reported to senior compliance management.

### OFAC-1.5 Blocking, rejecting and record retention

Banks should have clear procedures for the action to take once a true sanctions match is confirmed, including whether the property must be blocked or the transaction rejected. Procedures should identify who may authorise the action, how affected funds are held and how required reports to the authority are prepared and submitted on time. Banks should retain the supporting records of each confirmed match and its resolution in a form that can be retrieved promptly for supervisory review.

### OFAC-1.6 Sanctions exposure review for new markets

Before entering a new country, region or corridor, banks should assess how comprehensive and targeted sanctions programmes affect the proposed activity. The assessment should consider local counterparties, intermediaries, transit routes and the likelihood that customers may have links to restricted territories. Where the exposure cannot be managed with existing controls, the bank should add geographic screening rules, restrict permitted activity or decline to proceed, and should record the rationale for its decision.

## List types (SDN, sectoral)

### OFAC-2.1 Distinguishing blocking lists from sectoral lists

Banks should ensure that their screening programmes clearly distinguish between lists that require assets to be blocked and lists that impose narrower, activity-based restrictions. Procedures should explain how a match against each list type is handled, including whether the relationship must be frozen, the transaction rejected, or the activity permitted under conditions. Staff should be trained to recognise the difference, and escalation paths should reflect the different legal consequences of each outcome.

### OFAC-2.2 Ownership aggregation for blocked persons

Banks should design screening and due diligence so that entities owned in the aggregate by one or more listed persons are identified, even when the entity itself does not appear on any list. Institutions should collect ownership information sufficient to apply the relevant ownership threshold, consider combined holdings of several listed parties, and document how indirect ownership chains are assessed. Unresolved ownership questions should be escalated before an account is opened or a payment is released.

### OFAC-2.3 Handling sectoral list restrictions on debt and equity

Banks should establish controls for sectoral lists that restrict specific dealings, such as new debt beyond a stated maturity or new equity in named entities, rather than prohibiting all dealings. Screening hits against such entities should route to specialist reviewers who can assess the instrument, tenor and counterparties involved. Banks should record the rationale for each permitted or declined transaction and ensure product teams understand which features trigger a restriction.

### OFAC-2.4 Timely list updates and vendor data governance

Banks should ensure that changes to sanctions lists are reflected in screening systems promptly and that the update process is monitored for failures. Where a third-party vendor supplies list data or screening technology, the bank should verify completeness, test that new entries are ingested correctly, and retain accountability for results. Following each update, institutions should consider rescreening existing customers and in-flight transactions against the newly added names.

## 50% rule

### OFAC-3.1 Aggregating Ownership Across Blocked Persons

Banks should recognise that an entity may be treated as blocked when one or more sanctioned persons own, in the aggregate, half or more of it, even if no single owner reaches that level. Screening procedures should therefore add together the direct and indirect interests of all listed owners in a customer or counterparty, and should not rely on reviewing each owner in isolation. Where aggregate ownership cannot be determined, the bank should treat the uncertainty as a risk requiring escalation.

### OFAC-3.2 Collecting Ownership Data at Onboarding

Banks should gather enough ownership information at onboarding to support meaningful sanctions screening of entity customers. This includes the names and percentage holdings of owners above a defined threshold, plus intermediate holding companies in the chain. The bank should set the threshold below the level that triggers aggregation concerns, verify the information against reliable documents, and record where information was unavailable, so reviewers understand the limits of any screening result.

### OFAC-3.3 Looking Through Layered Ownership Structures

Banks should look through layered ownership structures, such as holding companies, trusts and nominee arrangements, to find sanctioned persons who hold interests indirectly. Where an intermediate entity is majority-owned by a sanctioned person, that entity's own holdings should be attributed to that person in full. Banks should also treat unexplained complexity, opaque jurisdictions and inconsistent ownership records as warning signs. They should seek clarification before establishing or continuing the relationship.

### OFAC-3.4 Rescreening After List and Ownership Changes

Banks should rescreen their customer base promptly when sanctions lists are updated and whenever ownership information on an entity changes. Rescreening should cover related entities that may now meet the ownership threshold, not just names that appear on the list. Alerts should be reviewed and dispositioned within defined timeframes, with the rationale documented. Confirmed matches should be escalated immediately so that blocking or rejection steps and required reporting can be carried out.
