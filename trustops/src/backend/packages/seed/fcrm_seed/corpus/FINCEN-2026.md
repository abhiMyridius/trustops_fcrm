## Risk assessment process

### FINCEN-1.1 Why Changes Trigger Risk Reassessment

Banks should reassess their financial-crime risk profile whenever a meaningful change alters who they serve, what they offer, where they operate or how they deliver services. A program that was sound when designed can become inadequate if new activity introduces exposures that existing controls were never built to address. Reassessment should therefore be treated as a standing part of change management, not an occasional exercise, so that control design keeps pace with the institution's actual risk.

### FINCEN-1.2 Scoping the Assessment to the Change

Banks should scope each risk assessment in proportion to the nature and scale of the proposed change. A minor adjustment to an existing workflow may need only a short review of affected controls, while a new product line or a new customer population may call for a full analysis of inherent risk, control effectiveness and residual exposure. Banks should document why a given depth of review was chosen so that reviewers can later understand the reasoning behind it.

### FINCEN-1.3 Evidence Used to Inform the Assessment

Banks should base their risk assessments on a range of credible evidence rather than on assumption alone. Useful inputs include internal transaction patterns, past suspicious activity reporting trends, findings from audits and testing, and national-level threat priorities published by authorities. Banks should record which sources were consulted, note any gaps in the available data, and explain how those limits affect confidence in the conclusions reached about the proposed change.

### FINCEN-1.4 Assessing Third-Party and Delivery Channel Risk

Banks should consider how a change in delivery method or reliance on an outside provider affects their exposure. When a vendor will perform or support monitoring, onboarding or payment activity, the assessment should examine the provider's controls, the data it will handle and the bank's ability to oversee it. Accountability for compliance remains with the bank, so outsourcing should never be treated as a transfer of responsibility for identified risks.

### FINCEN-1.5 Acting on Assessment Results and Review

Banks should ensure that assessment outcomes lead to concrete action. Where residual risk exceeds the institution's stated appetite, management should require additional controls, limit the scope of the change, or defer launch until gaps are closed. Results should be escalated to appropriate senior oversight, tracked to completion, and revisited after implementation to confirm that the controls operate as expected and that the original conclusions remain valid.

## Update on new products, markets, customer types

### FINCEN-2.1 Assess Financial Crime Risk Before Launching Products

Banks should assess the financial crime risk of a new product or a material feature change before it is offered to customers. The assessment should consider how the product could be used to move value, hide ownership, or layer transactions, and should identify the controls needed to manage that exposure. Launch approval should depend on those controls being designed, resourced, and ready to operate, rather than being added after problems appear.

### FINCEN-2.2 Reassess Risk When Entering New Markets

Banks should reassess their risk profile when they begin serving a new market or a new geographic area, whether directly or through counterparties. The review should weigh local illicit finance threats, the strength of oversight in that place, and the typical payment flows involved. Where the new exposure exceeds what current monitoring and screening can handle, the bank should strengthen those controls before activity begins and record the reasoning behind its decision.

### FINCEN-2.3 Evaluate New Customer Types Before Onboarding

Banks should evaluate the risks of any new customer type or segment before onboarding begins at scale. Examples include customers with complex ownership, unfamiliar business models, or unusual expected transaction patterns. The evaluation should determine whether existing due diligence questions, risk ratings, and expected-activity baselines remain suitable, and should adjust them where the new population behaves differently from the customers the program was originally built around.

### FINCEN-2.4 Review Process and Vendor Changes for Control Impact

Banks should review changes to operating processes, delivery channels, and third-party providers for their effect on the financial crime program. A new workflow, system, or outsourced service can weaken detection, create gaps in customer information, or shift accountability unclearly. The bank should test whether controls still work as intended after the change, confirm who is responsible for them, and keep evidence that the review took place and was approved.
