Read trustops/BUILD-PLAN.md “Status as built”, trustops/docs/governance/decision-log.md (ADR-01 to ADR-89) and trustops/docs/governance/known-gaps.md (dated 6 October) for what changed and what is open; Documents/07 to 13 describe the system as built.
What differs in the build
  • Nine agents, not five, each one Pydantic AI call on Bedrock and orchestrated by Caseflow (H1), not started by the analyst: intake reads each upload; screening runs after triage; research and assessment run in parallel; the critic reviews the assessment on a different model; the narrative agent drafts the completeness report and the clarification questions before the analyst reviews, and later the recommendation. The examiner answers from the audit chain on demand; watchlist runs when a jurisdiction list is published; policy_gap runs when the analyst notes a gap.
  • Not scripted: agent output is live on Bedrock, behind seven guards, and every proposal is accepted, edited or rejected by a person. Scores still come only from the deterministic engine.
  • “Mocked today” describes the prototype. The build has a database, authentication (X1 and OPA) and live retrieval.
  • Models: Amazon Bedrock only; no direct Anthropic or OpenAI keys, no LiteLLM, no GPT fallbacks (ADR-03). Tiers: cheap Haiku 4.5 → Nova Lite (screening, intake, watchlist); mid Sonnet 5.5 → Haiku 4.5 (narrative, examiner, policy_gap); strong Sonnet 5.5, retried on Sonnet 5.5, → Nova Pro (research, assessment); judge Nova Pro → Haiku 4.5 (critic and the eval judges). No Opus (ADR-62, ADR-65).
  • Since the concept: the analyst can ask the owner questions at any time (ADR-73), the owner starts from a New case form (ADR-75), jurisdictions are rated on one 1–5 scale (ADR-84), an intake job that fails before submit leaves the case with its owner rather than handing off (ADR-85), the analyst can deny a suggested planned control (ADR-87) and edit the recommendation’s conditions (ADR-88), and the committee reads a Summary and the Submission beside its Inbox and Vote (ADR-89).

The domain, in plain terms

Banks are legally obliged to understand how money launderers and sanctioned parties might exploit them. That obligation does not stop at the products they run today. It applies every time something changes.

If a bank launches a new product, enters a new country, hands part of a process to an outside company, or starts serving a customer type it has not served before, it has to assess whether that change creates a new route for financial crime. Regulators expect the assessment to exist, to be documented, and to be defensible years later.

The team that performs those assessments is FCRM, Financial Crimes Risk Management. The person proposing the change is usually a Product Owner on the business side. A Risk Committee signs off on the ones that carry real exposure.

Six kinds of change that require an assessment

New product · New feature · Process change · Vendor or third-party onboarding · New geography · New customer segment. A single change often falls into several at once, and the assessment covers the union of them.

How risk is actually measured

The industry method is two-stage, and the whole system is built around it.

1. Inherent risk

The exposure before any defences are considered. How likely is this to be exploited, and how bad would it be if it were?

2. Controls

The defences the bank already has. Sanctions screening, customer due diligence, transaction monitoring, vendor audits. Each has an effectiveness rating.

3. Residual risk

What is left after the controls are applied. This is the number that decides how much oversight the change needs.

Controls reduce risk. They never eliminate it. The system enforces a floor: no matter how strong the controls, at least 40 percent of inherent risk survives as residual. This is a real principle in the domain, and it is the reason the arithmetic is capped rather than open-ended.

Risk is assessed across four standard categories drawn from the US regulatory examination framework: customer or entity, product and service, geography, and delivery channel and third party. Which of the four apply to a given change depends on what kind of change it is.

The problem we are solving

The current process works, slowly and inconsistently. Three failures drive everything in the design.

Incomplete submissions

The business owner fills in a form without knowing what the analyst needs. The analyst then asks follow-up questions one at a time, by email, over days. This back and forth is the single largest contributor to the overall timeline.

Two analysts, two conclusions

The same change assessed by two people produces different answers. Policy is looked up from scratch each time, and control effectiveness is a judgment nobody has to evidence.

Unreconstructable reasoning

Months later, when a regulator asks why a decision was reached, the reasoning lives in email threads, personal spreadsheets and people's memories. Rebuilding it is manual archaeology.

Each of the three has a direct counterpart in the design: a completeness gate at intake, a shared policy layer with an evidenced scoring model, and a case record that accumulates continuously rather than being filed at the end.

The three roles

The prototype has three personas, and the boundaries between them are deliberate. Each sees a different subset of the same case.

RoleWhat they doWhat they deliberately cannot do
Product Owner
business side		 Raises the case, describes the change, answers the analyst's questions, receives the outcome. Cannot see the risk score, the policy mapping, the control ratings, or any of the assessment reasoning. Cannot configure the rules that decide whether their own change needs review.
FCRM Analyst
the assessment		 Reviews the submission, maps applicable policy, rates controls, produces the score and the recommendation. Owns all system configuration. Cannot edit what the business submitted. They request clarification; the business owner answers.
Risk Committee
oversight		 Reads the generated pack and casts one vote with a written rationale. Three members, majority decides. Cannot edit anything. Votes are hidden until all three are cast, so nobody votes on a running tally.

The flow we designed

A change moves through intake, assessment and decision. The governing principle throughout is that the system prepares and humans decide: every AI action is a proposal with a human accept or override, and the log of those decisions is the audit trail.

  1. Product Owner

    Case opens

    A case ID is issued at the start of the conversation, before any content exists. Everything attaches to it from that point.

  2. Product Owner · System

    Documents first, questions second

    The owner uploads what they already have. Extraction and classification run in parallel against it.

  3. Product Owner

    Categories confirmed

    Required fields depend on which categories apply, so this comes before detailed questions. Removing a proposed category needs a recorded reason.

  4. System · Product Owner

    Prior cases offered for reuse

    Field-level reuse with checkboxes, never a whole-case clone. Reused values keep a label naming their source case.

  5. System · Product Owner

    Only the gaps are asked about

    Answers go in the chat or an inline box, both writing to the same field. The completion ring shows the submission threshold.

  6. System

    Validation gate

    Four checks run visibly. Submission needs 80 percent of required fields and separately every always-required field. A failure names which ground it failed on and reopens intake asking only for what is missing.

  7. System

    Auto-triage assigns an analyst

    Workload-balanced rotation. Every case has a named owner from arrival.

  8. FCRM Analyst

    Review before running anything

    Summary, confirmed categories, flagged inconsistencies and precedent. No score is shown, because a number offered before the work anchors the assessment to it.

  9. FCRM Analyst

    Re-tag if the categories are wrong

    Adding a category expands the required set and can push a complete submission back below threshold.

  10. Analyst · Product Owner

    Clarification, batched and anchored

    One round rather than a trickle. Questions attach to fields, and marked answers write back as a new submission version with the original retained and the change shown as a diff.

  11. System

    Analysis pipeline

    Five agents run in sequence with the scoring computation between them, rendered distinctly because it is arithmetic rather than model output. Re-running discards prior work and requires a reason.

  12. FCRM Analyst

    Policy, risk factors and controls confirmed

    Clauses accepted, removed with a reason, or added by search. Control ratings confirmed in one click or overridden with a reason. Per component, never in bulk.

  13. System

    Score recomputes, band determines route

    Inspectable to four levels of depth, down to the arithmetic. An override that crosses a band boundary is flagged and logged with the before, the after and the cause.

  14. FCRM Analyst

    Recommendation confirmed and locked

    Unlocking later requires a reason and regenerates the pack. Material clarifications assemble themselves from what was asked and what changed.

  15. System · Analyst

    Committee pack generated

    Deterministic, no AI. Narrative and notes editable; numbers and ratings locked. The plain-language summary for the business owner must exist before the pack can be approved.

  16. Risk Committee

    Three votes, hidden until all are cast

    Mandatory written rationale each. Revealed together so the last voter judges the case rather than a result. Majority decides.

  17. Product Owner

    Outcome returns with a reason

    Decision and summary in business terms. Never the score, band, control ratings or policy clauses.

Filing is not a step. In the current process, someone drops documents on a shared drive at the end. Here the record accumulates as work happens: every agent action, every human override with its reason, every clarification round, every version of the submission, and the version of the scoring model, field set and policy corpus in force at the time.

The calculation

This is the part worth reading closely. It is deterministic arithmetic, it lives server-side in its own module, and no AI touches it. The agents supply inputs; the engine computes.

Inputs

InputRangeWhere it comes from
Likelihood1 to 5Set by the analyst against written definitions for each level. Some values are pre-populated from reference data, such as a jurisdiction risk list.
Impact1 to 5Set by the analyst against written definitions, informed by volume and customer exposure.
Control effectiveness0.0 to 10.0Per control, proposed by the system from a maintained inventory along with the evidence supporting it, then confirmed or overridden by the analyst.
Control weightsums to 1.00Held in the control inventory. Each risk category's controls carry weights summing to exactly one.
Category weightrelativeConfiguration. Normalised across whichever categories are in scope for the case, so they need not sum to one globally.

The chain

// per risk category in scope
Inherent   = Likelihood × Impact                    // 1 to 25
CE         = weighted mean of that category's control ratings
Retention  = 1 − (0.6 × CE ÷ 10)                    // 1.00 down to 0.40
Residual   = Inherent × Retention

// across all in-scope categories
Composite  = Σ (Residual × normalised category weight)
Band       = lookup(Composite) against the band table
Route      = the band's routing consequence

The retention formula is where the floor lives. At a control effectiveness of zero, retention is 1.00 and the full inherent risk carries through. At a perfect ten, retention is 0.40 and sixty percent of the risk has been mitigated, which is the most the model will ever allow.

Bands and routing

BandComposite residualRoute
Band 1 of 40.0 to 5.0Closes under the analyst's delegated authority
Band 2 of 45.1 to 10.0Closes under the analyst's delegated authority
Band 3 of 410.1 to 17.0Goes to committee review
Band 4 of 417.1 to 25.0Goes to committee review

Four bands over two routing outcomes is deliberate. The bands give reporting and query granularity finer than the routing decision itself, so a committee can see where in the range a case sits rather than only that it arrived.

Guards in the engine

Boundary proximity

When a composite lands within half a point of a band edge, the system flags it and requires the analyst to acknowledge before proceeding. A judgment-derived number of 10.0 against 10.1 changes who oversees the case, and that should be visible rather than silent.

Nothing is retroactive

Every case records the version of the scoring model, field set and policy corpus that applied to it. Changing configuration affects new work only. In-flight cases continue on their version, with an explicit logged option to re-score.

Evidence has a shelf life

Control ratings are backed by named artifacts with test dates. Evidence past its recency window is flagged stale; evidence approaching it is flagged as nearing. A rating with no supporting artifact is marked judgment-only.

Planned controls do not count

A control that does not exist yet is recorded separately and affects no score. The recommendation can reference it, but the arithmetic ignores it, because a control that is not live cannot mitigate anything.

Reviewing the controls, the analyst notices that the vendor due-diligence rating rests on an independent audit dated well before the corridor arrangement existed, covering the vendor's domestic operations only. They raise a clarification, confirm the audit's date and scope, and reduce that control's effectiveness from 7.6 to 4.5, recording the reason.

This is the whole design in one sequence. A gap in evidence surfaces, a clarification confirms it, a human overrides an AI proposal with a recorded reason, the arithmetic recomputes, the case crosses a routing boundary, and the change of oversight is logged with its cause. Every step of that is reconstructable months later.

Design rules that constrain the build

These are not preferences. Each one exists because violating it would undermine something the product claims.

AI proposes, humans decide

Every AI output is a proposal with an accept or override. Confirmation is per component, never bulk. A single accept-all button would make the audit trail record consent instead of review.

Scoring is arithmetic, not inference

Agents supply inputs and draft narrative. The engine computes every score. No model ever sets a rating or a band.

Policy is retrieved, never generated

Applicable clauses are shown verbatim with their source. If nothing applicable is found, the system says so rather than producing something plausible. A fabricated policy citation in a bank's risk record is worse than no citation.

The business owner cannot configure their own gate

Required fields, completion thresholds and classification rules are owned by FCRM and read-only to the business. The business owner controls only their own optional fields and notification preferences.

Submissions are never edited by the analyst

The analyst requests clarification; the business owner answers. Answers land in the submission as a new version with a visible diff. The original is retained permanently.

Overrides carry reasons

Removing a proposed category, removing a policy clause, changing a control rating and re-running an analysis all require a recorded reason. The reasons are the audit trail.

Visibility is asymmetric by design

The business owner never sees working scores, policy mapping or control ratings. Where those exist on a case, their view renders nothing at all rather than a locked placeholder.

Closed means closed

No analyst, including the one who signed it off, can re-run or edit a closed assessment. Silently changing the basis of a decision already delivered is the failure this prevents.

What is built, and what comes next

The prototype is a front end over a mock service layer. Understanding that boundary matters for anyone picking up the work.

Real today

The scoring engine is genuine deterministic arithmetic, not scripted output. Field resolution, band lookup, routing, version stamping, completeness calculation and all dashboard aggregation compute from a generated data corpus. The regulatory frameworks referenced are real ones.

Mocked today

All AI agent outputs are scripted. There is no database, no authentication, and no live retrieval. The persona switcher stands in for real sessions.

Architecture that anticipates this

Every data access goes through one service module with one function per backend call, each faking latency. Components never touch mock data directly. Replacing the mock with a real API is a change of function bodies, not call sites.

Sequence for making it real

Database and API first, then move the scoring engine server-side, then the agents one at a time. Policy retrieval is the first agent worth making real: it is the highest-value demonstration and its failure mode is visible rather than silent.

Glossary

Terms that appear throughout the code and the interface.

AML
Anti-money laundering. The body of law and practice requiring banks to detect and prevent the movement of criminal proceeds.
KYC and CDD
Know Your Customer and Customer Due Diligence. The checks a bank performs to establish who a customer is and what risk they carry.
FCRM
Financial Crimes Risk Management. The team that assesses and governs financial crime exposure.
Trigger category
One of the six kinds of change that require an assessment. A case can carry several, and its required fields are the union of the ones each category adds.
AML risk category
One of the four standard dimensions risk is assessed across: customer or entity, product and service, geography, delivery channel and third party. Distinct from trigger categories, and derived from them.
Inherent risk
Exposure before controls. Likelihood multiplied by impact, giving a value from 1 to 25.
Control
An existing mechanism that reduces risk. Screening systems, due diligence procedures, transaction limits, monitoring rules.
Control effectiveness
How well a control actually works, rated 0 to 10 and backed by named evidence with a test date.
Residual risk
What remains after controls are applied. The number that decides the level of oversight.
Band
The range the composite residual falls into, one of four. Determines routing and provides reporting granularity.
Delegated authority
The analyst's power to close a lower-risk case without committee involvement.
Always required
A field that must be filled regardless of the completion percentage. The mechanism that stops the threshold's slack landing on fields that determine the assessment.
Clarification round
One batched set of questions from analyst to business owner. Batching is enforced; questions do not go out individually.
SLA
The target turnaround, measured in business days. Tracked on two clocks: total elapsed, and analyst-active, which pauses while awaiting a response from the business.
FATF
Financial Action Task Force. The international body that publishes lists of jurisdictions with deficient anti-money-laundering regimes.
OFAC
The US Treasury office that administers sanctions programmes.
FFIEC
The US interagency body whose examination manual defines the four AML risk categories the scoring model uses.
